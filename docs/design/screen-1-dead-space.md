# Screen 1's dead space, and why nothing goes in it

**Decided 1 Aug 2026. Option A: ship it as it stands.**

Screen 1 is bottom-anchored on purpose. The cards sit in the thumb zone and the
space left over collects at the top. The design notes proposed filling that space
with a network schematic whose only bright element was a live green position dot.
The dot was deferred, so the schematic was never built, and Screen 1 sat at
"8.5/10 and NOT approved" for two weeks, blocking the entry-gate switch and with
it the Phase 2 exit criterion.

Three options were drawn at 360 x 640, the 3T's real layout size, rather than the
390 x 844 Figma frame that overflowed on 29 Jul.

## A. Ship it as it stands. CHOSEN

Zero lines. The rider gets the chip, the space, and three cards in the thumb
zone.

## B. Build the schematic, with the dot. DECLINED

The line drawn across the dead space with the rider's position glowing on it.

Declined for one reason, and it is not cost:

**The green position dot already exists on this screen.** It is in the status
chip, and it is not decoration there. A schematic dot would be a second answer to
a question the screen has already answered, in a second visual language, six
inches above the first one.

The frequency test settles it. A rider opens this screen twice a day, every
working day, for years. At that count anything that is not the two taps becomes
furniture, and furniture that restates a fact is worse than empty space, because
the eye still has to check it. Line context is the one thing a daily commuter on
the Central line never needs told.

**A correction, because the first version of this argument was wrong.** It also
claimed the schematic would push a third recent card out of the thumb zone. That
was asserted, never measured, and it is false: the column has about 242 dp of
dead space and the schematic needs about 102. It fits with all three cards, and
it would have fitted before the buttons were trimmed too. B is cheaper than it
was first made to sound. It is declined on duplication alone.

## C. Give the space the one fact. FALLBACK

Promote the chip's sentence to typography, so the emptiness reads as composed
rather than unfinished. No new mechanism and no new data.

Held as the fallback rather than chosen, because it costs something real: the
chip's tap is the GPS retry, and a heading does not read as pressable. That
retry is not cosmetic. One 8 second fix at launch is a coin flip indoors on an
old phone, which is exactly why a miss must never read as a final verdict.

## The honest read on the 8.5

Screen 1 scores low judged as a picture and high judged as a launcher. It has
one job, to start a journey in two taps, and it already does that job with a
fully composed bottom half. The 0.5 was never missing.

## What this decision does not cover

The third state, saved routes, is still undrawn and unbuilt: `SavedRoute` has no
model and no table, and it is a separate blocker.
